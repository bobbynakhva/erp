Tamba ERP is manufacturing ERP software for brass component factories that need to connect material movements, production work orders, purchasing, subcontracting, inspection and dispatch. Built with input from working factories in Jamnagar, Tamba also brings sales, cash flow and GST reports into the same operational environment.

What should ERP connect in a brass parts factory?

A brass-parts order often involves more than one department. Purchasing arranges material, stores records its movement, production organizes the work, and the team coordinates any outside processing before inspection and dispatch. An ERP should help those teams work from connected records instead of repeatedly reconstructing the job's status.

Tamba's core modules cover these operational areas. Process details differ between factories, so the right starting point is to map your actual material, production and dispatch flow—not assume a standard configuration fits every component.

How Tamba supports the manufacturing workflow

Inventory, material movements and purchasing

Use inventory and material-movement workflows alongside purchasing. During setup, agree how your team records items, units of measure, stores and material issues. Consistent master data is important when different people purchase, issue and receive the material needed for a job.

Production planning and work orders

Bring planning and production orders into the ERP. Connect the discussion about what needs to be made with the material and labour involved. Review the way your factory schedules and records work before deciding how to configure the production workflow.

Subcontracting and inspection

Subcontracting and inspection are included among Tamba's capabilities. If a brass component leaves your premises for outside processing, map the handoff and return with your team. Confirm that your required checks and documents fit the available workflows rather than assuming every specialist process is already automated.

Sales, delivery challans and GST reports

Coordinate sales and labour work with delivery challans and the business's financial reporting. Tamba includes cash flow and GST reports. Your accountant should review statutory reporting and filing requirements; software features do not replace tax or compliance advice.

Why Jamnagar factory experience matters

Tamba was built with the help of real working factories in Jamnagar. That practical input informs the focus on material movements, work orders, subcontracting and dispatch. The aim is a connected view of factory work, not an isolated screen for each department.

The software is also intended for manufacturers outside Jamnagar. Suitability depends on your operational requirements, not only your location or the material you use. Discuss your actual production flow with the product team before committing to a setup.

What should you prepare before setting up ERP?

  1. Map one representative order. Follow it from purchasing and material receipt through production, outside work, inspection and dispatch.
  2. Agree your master data. Prepare the company information, items, units, stores, customers and suppliers your team needs.
  3. Identify who owns each step. Decide which staff handle company administration, stores, production and sales.
  4. Review documents and reporting. List the delivery documents, inspection checks and GST reports the factory uses, then confirm the fit with the product team.

How does your factory get access?

Each manufacturer uses its own ERP deployment and database at a company-specific domain. A manufacturer administrator completes the first-time company setup. Employees use that company's ERP address; the public Tamba website is for learning about the product, not signing into a factory.

Tamba does not offer public self-service signup or automatic workspace provisioning through this page. Contact the product team to discuss your factory's requirements and access arrangements.
